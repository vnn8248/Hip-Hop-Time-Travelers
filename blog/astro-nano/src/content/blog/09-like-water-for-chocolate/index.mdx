Common's fourth album landed in March 2000 as a hard turn away from the
boom-bap of his Chicago come-up and toward the loose, live-feeling sound the
Soulquarians were building at Electric Lady Studios. J Dilla anchors most of
the record's production, with James Poyser, Questlove, and the rest of the
collective filling in the spaces around him. It's an album that sounds like a
crate being dug through in real time — and, twenty-plus years on, it's become
part of the crate itself.

That makes it a good record to look at three ways: who made each track, what
each track pulled from the past, and what shape the album takes song to song.
