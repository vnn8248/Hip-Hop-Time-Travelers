Facts and Stories

4

Common's song 'The Light' is framed as a love letter, addressed to his partner at the time Erykah Badu (Badu also appeared in the song's music video). On the lyric "love ain't simple," Common explained what he meant by this was that despite love being "easy to give" and it being "so natural for us," sometimes "fears and different things can get in the way." "They say fear and love can't exist in the same space," he concluded, "so you've gotta let love overcome that."

Common also said that when he wrote the line "it don't take a whole day to recognize sunshine" he knew it was a good lyric, since for him it spoke to the truth that when you fall for someone, it's not something you have to think over, "you just know it."

Common has described the "change in [his] career" that his song 'The Light' brought about due to the song's commercial success. The song peaked at No. 44 on the Billboard Hot 100 and remains his highest charting single. Common recounted how for the first time in career, people would come up to him to tell him how much they loved the song. Despite being hugely grateful for the response, he admitted he was surprised that it was this song in particular which has resonated so strongly with people, since when he and J Dilla made the song they hadn't set out to make a "commercial joint."

In an interview for the Juan Epstein podcast alongside producer Pete Rock to promote the duo's collaborative album 'The Auditorium Vol. 1', Common spoke on the making of and release of The Light, revealing that Questlove tried to talk him out of including the track on the J Dilla produced 'Like Water for Chocolate' because it didn't fit with the rest of the tracks which featured beats more commonly associated with Dilla's signature sound.

Common also revealed that Bobby Caldwell demanded 100% of the publishing rights to clear the sample, but commented “let me say this: he deserved 100%”.

Caldwell's wife Mary Caldwell later stated in an interview that her late husband in fact only claimed 12.5% of the publishing in the song, referring to information available on BMI.
